# Companion works (public domain)

*The Working Bee* is the primary style source. These books sit in the same lane: educational, stern, anti-hype, about labor, class, and how institutions actually work. We can pull them from Project Gutenberg without a separate novel scrape.

| Work | Why it matches | Gutenberg |
|---|---|---|
| Thorstein Veblen, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899) | Desk/sale culture, status as labor, cutting and educational | [833](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/833) |
| John Ruskin, *Unto This Last* (1862) | Value, wages, what work is for; moral without affirmation-speak | [36541](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36541) |
| Thomas Carlyle, *Past and Present* (1843) | Captains of industry, idle vs working order; oratorical like the Bee | [26159](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/26159) |
| Henry George, *Progress and Poverty* (1879) | Why labor does not keep what it builds | [55308](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/55308) |
| Jack London, *The People of the Abyss* (1903) | Direct reportage of working life; no pep | [1688](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1688) |
| Jacob Riis, *How the Other Half Lives* (1890) | Educational, visual, unsentimental | [45502](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45502) |

**Skip for voice:** self-help, success manuals, *Up From Slavery*-style uplift, and most modern “hustle” books.

Primary style: *The Working Bee*.  
Missing ending (content, not voice): *The Unpaid Hour* — reclaim one hour from the hive, rewritten in the Bee’s register, not as wellness protocol.

Download companions:

```bash
python scripts/fetch_companions.py
```

Files land in `data/companion/`.
